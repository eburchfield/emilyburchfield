Farmers don't choose alone. Buyers, gins, elevators, processors, lenders, crop insurance and Extension all shape what a farm can grow and sell. When these options widen, farms and the places around them have somewhere new to go. We trace how they open and close, with Georgia as our home base.

## Levers for Georgia's food system

In the [Georgia Farm & Food Initiative](/research/futures-together/)'s listening sessions, farmers and food system leaders named what it would take to keep more Georgia-grown food in Georgia and keep the farms growing it in business. Seven priorities came up across regions:

* **Farm viability and profitability:** fair prices, reliable markets and the ability to make a living farming.
* **Missing-middle infrastructure:** cold storage, aggregation, processing and distribution sized for farms selling to local and regional markets.
* **Reliable local and regional markets:** institutional, wholesale, retail and direct buyers of Georgia-grown food.
* **Capital that reaches the ground:** flexible funding and fair lending.
* **Trusted coordination and knowledge:** shared directories, peer learning and fewer duplicated efforts.
* **Farmer and farmworker voice:** structured channels to state and federal decision-makers.
* **Policy-ready evidence:** data and research that show what works and how it can scale.

Our research follows these levers: where they are missing, what makes them work, and which policies could open new pathways for farms and the communities around them ([Magliocca et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1088/2976-601X/adeca9)).
